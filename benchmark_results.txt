Notes:
- The percentages in "Averages" are how many fewer nodes astar/bidijkstra expanded vs
  dijkstra to solve the same query.
- All three algorithms stop as soon as the search is provably done (target popped for
  dijkstra/astar; frontiers can't improve the best meeting point for bidijkstra) rather
  than running to completion over the whole graph, so nodes_expanded reflects real work
  done per query, not a fixed cost proportional to graph size.
- The "(Nx)" figures next to astar/bidijkstra's average time are wall-clock speedup vs
  dijkstra (dijkstra_us / that algorithm's us), from the same process/run.
- *_us columns are each the mean of 25 repeated runs of that exact query, not a single
  timing - this toolchain's steady_clock has ~1ms resolution, too coarse to time one
  sub-millisecond query directly, so timings are averaged to rise above that noise floor.
- nodes_expanded is exact (not timing-dependent) and is the more reliable metric here:
  wall-clock speedup can be inconsistent or even negative despite large node-count cuts,
  because astar pays a real per-node cost (a haversine heuristic call per relaxed edge)
  that dijkstra doesn't, and bidijkstra pays for running two priority queues and a
  meeting check instead of one - the win only shows up on the clock once the nodes saved
  outweigh that per-node overhead. See NOTES.md for the full discussion.
